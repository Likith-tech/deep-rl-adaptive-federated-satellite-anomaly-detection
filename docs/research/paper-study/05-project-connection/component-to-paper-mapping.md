# Component-to-Paper Mapping

| Our component | Relevant paper(s) | What we learn from them |
|---|---|---|
| Satellite telemetry anomaly detection (Track B, OPSSAT-AD) | Paper 3 (Hundman), Paper 1 (Yang), Paper 2 (Maleki Sadr) | The general recipe (forecast → residual → threshold), the value of attention for long-range temporal patterns, and the idea of adding uncertainty estimation — none directly implemented as-is in your project, but the conceptual lineage behind why a GRU+attention architecture is a reasonable choice for Track B |
| Network intrusion / "satellite network" anomaly detection (Track A, NSL-KDD-simulated clients) | Paper 4 (Chen/FedSTAR) | The domain framing (classification of malicious vs. normal traffic across distributed clients) and the general federated + DRL system shape — your project's Track A is architecturally closer to this paper's problem than to Papers 1–3's |
| Deep learning model choice | Paper 1 (attention), Paper 2 (Bayesian/MC-dropout), Paper 4 (SimVPv2/gSTA/LKA) | Three different ways to make a temporal deep learning model "smarter" (attention, uncertainty, spatio-temporal coupling) — useful reference points if your project's report needs to justify why its own models (BaselineMLP, TemporalGRUAttention) are comparatively simple by deliberate choice |
| Federated learning (FedAvg, Phase 6) | Paper 4 | Confirms FedAvg is the standard, expected starting point before any adaptive mechanism — your project's Phase 6 IS exactly this |
| Non-IID handling / adaptive weighting (Phase 8, Phase 9B) | Paper 4 | The clearest evidence in your literature that plain FedAvg struggles under Non-IID data, and that *some* adaptive mechanism (rule-based or learned) is needed — Paper 4 solves it with SAC+clustering; your project independently solves the same underlying problem with a rule (Phase 8) then a DQN (Phase 9B) |
| Reinforcement learning / DRL controller (Phase 9B) | Paper 4 | The only paper in your set with any RL — the natural comparison point for justifying DQN vs. SAC, and for framing your project's own honestly-reported negative result |
| Privacy / server-client boundary | Paper 4 (states the FL privacy motivation) | Confirms privacy is a standard, expected justification for FL in this space; your project goes further by actually testing the boundary (AST-based tests), which none of the 4 papers describe doing |
| Dataset choice / Track A vs. Track B split | Paper 4 (uses benchmark, not real, satellite data) and Papers 1–3 (use real, but single-spacecraft, telemetry) | Confirms that "no real multi-satellite federated dataset exists publicly" is a field-wide reality, not a shortcoming unique to your project — justifies your project's own two-track approach as a reasonable, honestly-scoped response to a genuine data-availability gap |
| Reward design (Phase 9B) | Paper 4 (accuracy gain − degradation penalty − divergence penalty) | A directly comparable reward-shaping pattern: both Paper 4 and your project build a multi-term reward balancing performance against a "don't destabilize/diverge" penalty — useful for explaining your own reward formula (`ΔF1 − λ_fair·perf_spread − λ_stab·1[action changed]`) by direct structural analogy |
